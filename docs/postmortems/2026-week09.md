## Root causes

### 1. The rules weren't in the system (primary)
The charter said: no double-digit dog in a 5-point slot; no 5-pointer resting on an
unverified cause; Pick 1 = the highest-probability pick. The engine knew none of that.
`five_eligible` only looked at tags (`qb_uncertain`, `model_only` and so on), and since the
Nov 7 change, `big_move` didn't block a 5 slot either. Every rule had to be applied by a
person at 10 AM on a Saturday, and none was.

### 2. The engine rated unexplained big-dog moves at 58–60%
Three of the five picks (MRSH, WYO, UTEP) had "cause unverified" written in their own notes.
The engine priced MRSH at 0.600, the highest on the board, purely because the market had
moved 3 points toward it. The calibration table maps a 2–3 point edge to a 58.6% cover rate,
and on Nov 7 the `big_move` 3-point cap was turned off because the backtest said big moves
toward us covered 63% in 2026.

### 3. Suspected lookahead in that backtest finding
The backtest measures the move to the **closing** line, which forms after our noon deadline
and includes news we can't see at noon. When a line moves our way by the close, it's usually
because something real happened, which makes "big move toward us" look great in hindsight.
At noon, before the cause is known, the same move is much weaker evidence. The Nov 7 change
was probably fitted to information we never have at decision time. **Unproven, but it's the
most likely explanation for why the engine loved MRSH.** It needs re-testing on the noon
odds snapshots (collected since Nov 7) before `big_move` is trusted again.

### 4. Too many cards, no single owner of "final"
Five versions in three hours, each internally reasonable. When every card is "a draft,"
whichever one is on screen last gets sent. Our own work cap (one sweep, one judge, one
morning check) was blown through.

### 5. The leader-swing rule wasn't applied to the sent card
Stein's card was pulled at 7:45 AM, and TENN -5.5 was flagged as a direct swing against his
KENTUCKY +5.5. It was on four of the five chat cards. The engine card didn't include it, and
nothing compared the sent card against Stein's.

### What was *not* the cause
- **The Michigan pick.** A real 2-point number edge that closed at -14/-14.5; the market agreed
  with us. Its risk (a 120th-ranked offense on a third-string QB) was written down. A 55% pick
  loses 45% of the time. That's variance, though it shouldn't have been paired in the 5 slots
  with another fragile pick.
- **Data quality.** Every line matched the PDF, and the market data was a 7-book median.

## What went right
- The sweep and the judge correctly found the real edges (MICH, WYO, TENN, USA) and cut the
  stale ones (Colts +5.5 was dead, and the Northwestern line had flipped).
- The leader's card was pulled before lock, so the TENN swing was known.
- The PR review on the fix caught four real holes (wrong team passing, a failed card labeled
  sendable, slot renumbering, correlation) before merge.

## Verification

Replaying Week 9 with engine v2 (`test_card_gate.py`):

- The sent card **fails** the gate: `Pick 1 (5 pts): 37 MRSH +10.5 can't be a 5-pointer: dog +10.5 (>= +10)`.
- v2's own card: `1. 55 MICH -12.5 · 2. 46 USA +8.5 · 3. 37 MRSH +10.5 · 4. 63 WYO +6.5 · 5. 61 UTEP +9.5`, which **passes**.
- Counterfactual so far: USA +8.5 covered (5 points instead of MRSH's 0), and Marshall's loss
  drops from 5 points to 3. **That's a +5 swing on the 5-point slots.** The gate is a floor; it
  doesn't make MRSH or UTEP good picks, which is why action 5 matters.

## Lessons
1. **A rule that isn't in the code isn't a rule.** The fix is never another paragraph in the charter.
2. **An edge you can't explain is not an edge you can bet in a 5 slot.**
3. **Check the backtest for lookahead whenever it turns off a safety rule.**
4. **One final card, one gate, one sender.**
